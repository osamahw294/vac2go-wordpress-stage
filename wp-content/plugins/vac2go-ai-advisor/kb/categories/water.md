# Water

## Summary

Vac2Go rents one water truck: the Load King 4,000-gallon water truck, available at all Vac2Go branches, in one standard configuration (front, side and rear air-operated spray bars, a 50 ft hose reel with a fireman's nozzle, a shaft-driven pump, a hydrant fill hose and a pond/creek suction kit; no water cannon). It is used for dust suppression, soil compaction, site and equipment washdown, and on-site water supply. It is non-potable only: never for drinking water or food-grade use, and clean-water service only (no wastewater, sludge or contaminated liquids, which belong in a vacuum or liquid vacuum truck). A Class B CDL is required. The customer supplies the water, normally from a hydrant. Vac2Go's water trucks are never a refill source for hydro excavators or combination units; suggest one only when the customer describes dust, compaction or washdown needs. For more than 4,000 gallons delivered to one point, the customer needs a tanker or frac tank from another provider.

## Questions and answers

From Vac2Go's Water Q&A (2026-10-15). Answer from these, keeping their meaning; where an answer gives a general figure and the unit card gives its own figure, use the unit card's figure.

### Group A: Water, high level

**Q: What is a water truck in Vac2Go's fleet, and what configurations are available: spray bars, cannons, hose reels, onboard pumps?**

Vac2Go rents the Load King 4,000-gallon water truck in one standard configuration: front, side, and rear air-operated spray bars; a 50 ft x 1-1/2 in. manual-rewind hose reel with a fireman's nozzle; a shaft-driven 4x3 water pump; a 2-1/2 in. x 50 ft hydrant fill hose; and a 3 in. pond/creek suction kit. No water cannon.

**Q: What kinds of jobs are water trucks used for: dust suppression, compaction, washdown, fire protection, supplying water to other equipment?**

Dust suppression, soil compaction, site and equipment washdown, and on-site water supply. The fireman's nozzle can provide basic fire support, but the truck is not a firefighting unit and should not be presented as one.

**Q: Which industries most commonly rent them?**

Construction and site prep, utility, municipal/government, environmental/landfill, and industrial.

**Q: Is the fleet potable-rated, non-potable, or both? Can a water truck be used for drinking water or food-grade applications?**

Non-potable only. The truck can never be used for drinking water or food-grade applications.

**Q: How is a water truck different from a tanker? Can a tanker be used to haul water, and if so when would you use one over the other?**

A water truck distributes water across a site with spray bars and a hose. A tanker hauls bulk water to a single point. Vac2Go rents water trucks only; for more than 4,000 gallons delivered to one point, the customer needs a tanker or frac tank from another provider.

**Q: Do water trucks commonly support other Vac2Go units on site, refilling a hydro excavator or a combination unit, for example? Is that a standard pairing the advisor should suggest?**

No. Vac2Go does not support truck-to-truck water transfer, so the advisor should not suggest a water truck as a refill source for hydro excavators or combination units.

**Q: What capacities does Vac2Go stock?**

4,000 gallons, available at all Vac2Go branches. A Class B CDL is required to operate it.

**Q: Who supplies the water, and does Vac2Go help the customer arrange a fill source?**

The customer supplies the water. The truck ships with a hydrant hose (a hydrant meter or permit from the local water utility is usually required) and a pond/creek suction kit. Hydrant fill is the standard method. Pond or creek fill is a backup: the truck has no primer pump, so the operator must first flood the pump and suction hose, usually with water already in the tank, before it will draft.

**Q: What jobs is a water truck not the right choice for?**

Drinking water; wastewater, sludge, or contaminated liquids (redirect to a vacuum truck or liquid vacuum truck); refilling other Vac2Go units; bulk hauling over 4,000 gallons; and customers without a CDL driver.

**Q: What are the most common reasons customers rent a water truck?**

Dust control, compaction water, and a water supply on sites without a hydrant.

### Group D: Water quality and contents: Water

**Q: What can be carried in a water truck besides plain water: dust suppressants, soil stabilizers, chlorinated water, reclaimed or gray water?**

Customers may use dust suppressants, soil stabilizers, and reclaimed or gray water. The truck must be cleaned and returned with a clean certificate.

**Q: Can it carry non-potable or reclaimed water? Any restrictions on where that water can be applied?**

Yes. The customer is responsible for following local rules on where non-potable or reclaimed water can be applied.

**Q: Can it carry heated water?**

No. The truck has no heating system.

**Q: Can it carry chemically treated water: polymer, chloride, surfactant? Does that affect tank compatibility or require a dedicated unit?**

Allowed. The truck must be cleaned and returned with a clean certificate.

**Q: Can a water truck ever be used for wastewater or contaminated water, or is the fleet strictly clean-water service?**

No. Water trucks are clean-water service only. Contaminated liquids belong in a vacuum truck or liquid vacuum truck.

**Q: Is there a cross-contamination policy? Can a unit that has carried treated water return to potable service, and what does that involve?**

Every truck must be cleaned and returned with a clean certificate. Return to potable service does not apply because no units are potable.

**Q: Are there materials that should never be put into a water truck?**

Fuel, oils, solvents, acids, sewage, hazardous waste, or anything flammable.

### Group E: Sizing and "is this the right truck": Water

**Q: How do I know if I need a water truck vs. a tanker vs. a portable water tank?**

Water truck to spread water across a site. Tanker to deliver bulk water to one point. Portable or frac tank to keep water staged on site for days. Vac2Go rents the water truck only.

**Q: What drives the sizing decision: tank capacity, pump and spray output, or the number of refill trips per shift?**

With one size in the fleet, the question is how many trucks the job needs. That depends on site size, site conditions, job duration, and distance to the fill source.

**Q: For dust suppression on a site of a given size, what is the rule of thumb for capacity and refill frequency?**

The advisor should collect site size, job duration, and distance to the fill source, then route the customer to their Vac2Go branch for a recommendation rather than quoting numbers.

**Q: When would Vac2Go recommend a water truck alongside a hydro excavator or combination unit rather than relying on that unit's own water tank?**

Only when the job has a separate dust control, compaction, or washdown need. The water truck is never positioned as a refill source for a hydro excavator or combination unit.

**Q: Is a water truck usually a standalone rental or a support rental? Should the advisor surface it proactively when it recommends a hydro excavator or combination unit?**

A standalone rental. The advisor should surface it only when the customer describes dust, compaction, or washdown needs, not proactively with every hydro excavator or combination unit recommendation.
