# Industrial Vacuum

## Summary

An industrial vacuum truck (air mover, vacuum loader) is a heavy-duty vacuum system that removes, recovers and transports wet, dry and slurry material through large-diameter hoses into an onboard debris tank. Typical jobs: plant and refinery shutdowns, tank, silo and bin cleanouts, pit, sump and trench cleaning, spill cleanup, dry bulk loading, catalyst removal, ash and dust removal, ready-mix silt traps, micro trenching, roof ballast and blast media. It handles powders, fly ash, cement dust, sand, gravel, grain, pellets, sludge and slurry. Choose it when the goal is to remove or transfer material rather than excavate: a hydro excavator is better for daylighting and digging around utilities, a combination unit for jetting sewer and storm lines, and a liquid vacuum or liquid ring unit for large volumes of liquid, wet waste or flammable material. Hazardous, combustible, hot, acidic or corrosive material needs evaluation by Vac2Go before rental; radioactive material and asbestos are not suitable.

### Group A: Industrial Vacuum Trucks (High Level)

**Q: What is an industrial vacuum truck?**

An industrial vacuum truck is a heavy-duty vacuum system designed to safely remove, recover, and transport a wide range of wet, dry, and slurry materials. These trucks generate powerful vacuum airflow that moves material through large-diameter hoses into an onboard debris tank, making them ideal for industrial cleaning, maintenance, spill response, and material recovery.

**Q: Can industrial vacuum trucks handle both wet and dry material?**

Yes. One of the biggest advantages of an industrial vacuum truck is its ability to handle wet materials, dry materials, sludge, slurry, powders, granules, and many other industrial waste streams with the same machine (depending on the material and configuration).

**Q: How is an industrial vacuum truck different from a hydro excavator?**

A hydro excavator combines high-pressure water with a vacuum system to safely expose underground utilities through excavation. An industrial vacuum truck is designed primarily to vacuum, recover, and transport material rather than excavate with water. While an industrial vacuum can remove material from an excavation, it is generally the better choice when excavation is not required, and maximum vacuum performance is needed.

**Q: How is it different from a combination/sewer cleaning unit?**

Combination sewer cleaners are purpose-built for cleaning sewer and storm drain systems using high-pressure water jetting and vacuum recovery. Industrial vacuum trucks are designed for broader industrial applications and excel at removing heavy debris, dry bulk materials, sludge, process waste, and other materials found in industrial facilities, not just sewer infrastructure.

**Q: How is it different from a liquid vacuum truck?**

A liquid vacuum truck is primarily designed to collect and transport liquids and pumpable waste. Industrial vacuum trucks are built to handle a much wider range of materials, including dry solids, powders, heavy sludge, bulk debris, and mixed wet/dry waste that many liquid vacuum units cannot efficiently recover.

**Q: When would I need an industrial vacuum truck instead of a hydro excavator?**

Choose an industrial vacuum truck when your goal is to remove, recover, or transfer material rather than excavate. It’s the preferred solution for plant cleanouts, tank cleaning, spill recovery, material transfer, shutdown maintenance, and removing accumulated solids, sludge, or dry bulk materials.

**Q: What materials can an industrial vacuum handle that other trucks can’t?**

Industrial vacuum trucks are capable of handling materials such as:
- Dry powders
- Fly ash
- Cement dust
- Sand
- Gravel
- Catalyst (could be flammable or combustible)
- Grain
- Plastic pellets
- Coal fines (could be combustible)
- Sludge
- Slurry
- Filter media
- Scale
- Process waste
- Wet and dry industrial debris
Their high airflow and vacuum capability make them especially effective for materials that are difficult to shovel, convey, or load manually.

**Q: What jobs is an industrial vacuum truck not the right application for?**

Industrial vacuum trucks may not be the best option for:
- Precision utility locating or daylighting (hydro excavation is better)
- High-pressure sewer line jetting (combination sewer cleaners are designed for this)
- Transporting large volumes of clean liquids where only pumping is required (liquid vacuum trucks are often more efficient)
- Heavy excavation requiring digging equipment
- Materials that are hazardous or incompatible with the specific vacuum system without the proper configuration or safety controls
- Radioactive material
- Removal of Asbestos

- Specialized vacuum equipment for a specific project

### Group D: Material Handling (Industrial Vacuum)

**Q: What is the maximum particle size an industrial vacuum can handle?**

The maximum particle size depends on the truck model, hose diameter, and the material being recovered. Industrial vacuum trucks are designed to handle a wide range of materials, from fine powders and dust to gravel, aggregate, and other coarse solids. If your material contains oversized debris, our team can help determine whether it is suitable for vacuum recovery.

**Q: What weight of material can the unit pick up?**

Industrial vacuum trucks are designed to recover heavy materials such as sand, gravel, sludge, wet soil, catalyst, and industrial debris. Actual lifting capability depends on the material’s density, moisture content, hose length, vertical lift, and distance from the truck.

**Q: Can it handle abrasive materials?**

Yes. Industrial vacuum trucks are commonly used to recover abrasive materials such as sand, grit, fly ash, cement dust, blast media, mill scale, and aggregate. Because abrasive materials increase equipment wear, the truck may require specialized liners, wear components, or operating procedures depending on the application.

**Q: Can it handle hot materials? What are the temperature limits?**

It depends. Industrial vacuum trucks can often handle warm materials, but maximum allowable temperatures vary by truck configuration, hose materials, filters, seals, and debris tank components.
Material should always be allowed to cool or evaluated before vacuuming. If your application involves elevated temperatures, please contact Vac2Go so we can verify compatibility before rental.

**Q: Can it handle hazardous or regulated materials?**

Possibly, but only with the proper equipment and safety procedures.
Whether a material can be recovered depends on factors such as:
- The type of hazardous material
- Regulatory requirements
- The truck configuration
- Disposal requirements
- Customer safety procedures

**Q: Can it handle flammable or combustible materials?**

Some flammable or combustible materials require specialized equipment and operating procedures. Material characteristics, dust explosibility, ignition risk, and site safety requirements must all be evaluated before vacuuming.
Always discuss these applications with Vac2Go before renting equipment

**Q: Can it handle acidic materials?**

It depends on the type of acid, concentration, temperature, and compatibility with the truck’s tank, hoses, valves, and seals.
Some corrosive materials require specialized equipment or may not be suitable for standard industrial vacuum trucks. Our team can help determine the safest solution.

**Q: Can it handle slurry at high temperature (100°C / 212°F and above)?**

Generally, very high-temperature slurry requires special evaluation.
Temperature affects hoses, seals, filters, tank coatings, and other components. If your material approaches or exceeds 100°C (212°F), contact Vac2Go before rental so we can verify equipment compatibility.

**Q: Can it handle dusty fines like Black Beauty® or coal dust?**

Yes, industrial vacuum trucks are commonly used to recover fine industrial materials such as:
- Coal dust
- Fly ash
- Black Beauty® blasting media
- Cement dust
- Lime
- Carbon black (application dependent)
- Powdered products
However, some fine dusts may present combustible dust hazards and require additional safety precautions. Tell us about your material so we can recommend the appropriate equipment.

**Q: What materials should never be vacuumed with an industrial vacuum truck?**

Certain materials should not be vacuumed with a standard industrial vacuum truck unless the equipment is specifically designed and approved for the application. These may include:
- Explosive or unstable materials
- Radioactive materials
- Materials that exceed the truck’s temperature limits
- Materials that are chemically incompatible with the truck’s construction
- Certain highly corrosive chemicals
- Materials that could ignite or react inside the vacuum system
- Materials prohibited by local, state, or federal regulations
Advisor note: Do not give hard limits for particle size, temperature or lifting weight unless a unit card gives that unit's figure; these depend on the blower, hose size, lift height, vacuum level and material. Say it depends on the configuration.

### Group E: Sizing & “Is This the Right Truck?”

**Q: How do I know if I need an industrial vacuum instead of a smaller unit?**

An industrial vacuum truck is typically the right choice when you need to:
- Move large volumes of dry material.
- Vacuum heavy or dense materials.
- Reach long hose distances.
- Vacuum from elevated structures such as silos, baghouses, or hoppers.
- Work continuously for extended periods.
- Minimize downtime on large industrial cleanout projects.
Smaller trailer or portable vacuums are often better for maintenance work, confined spaces, or jobs involving relatively small amounts of material.

**Q: What’s the sizing logic when a job is on the borderline between categories?**

Vac2Go generally looks at several factors:
- Total material volume.
- Material weight and density.
- Hose length required.
- Vertical lift.
- Distance from the truck to the work area.
- How quickly the job needs to be completed.
- Whether material needs to be transported after collection.
If several of these factors are demanding, the larger industrial vacuum truck is usually the better choice because it finishes the work faster and more efficiently.

**Q: When would Vac2Go recommend a hydro excavator instead of an industrial vacuum?**

A hydro excavator is recommended when the primary objective is safely exposing buried utilities.
Typical hydro excavation jobs include:
- Daylighting utilities.
- Pole and sign installation.
- Potholing.
- Utility verification.
- Excavating around gas, electric, fiber optic, or water lines.
- Precision digging with minimal ground disturbance.
- Demolition Operations
- Bridge Guard Railing Construction
- Interstate Construction
If the job is primarily excavation, choose a hydro excavator.
If the job is primarily material removal or industrial cleaning, choose an industrial vacuum.

**Q: When would Vac2Go recommend a combination unit instead?**

A combination sewer truck is usually recommended when the work requires both:
- High-pressure water jetting, and
- Vacuum recovery.
- Storm Drain Inspections
Examples include:
- Sewer line cleaning.
- Storm drain cleaning.
- Catch basin cleaning.
- Culvert cleaning.
- Lift station cleaning.
- Pipeline maintenance.
If you’re cleaning infrastructure with water and immediately vacuuming the debris, a combination unit is typically the best solution.

**Q: When would Vac2Go recommend a liquid vacuum or liquid ring unit instead?**

A liquid vacuum or liquid ring unit is generally recommended when handling:
- Large volumes of liquids or sludge.
- Wet industrial waste.
- Tank cleanouts.
- Oil and water mixtures.
- Materials that require continuous wet vacuum service.
- Flammable or Combustible Materials
Liquid ring systems perform well in wet environments and are often selected when moisture would reduce the efficiency of a dry industrial vacuum

## How it compares (from Vac2Go's Round 2 answers)

- **vs. hydro excavator:** a hydro excavator actively loosens and excavates the ground; a standard dry industrial vacuum can only recover material that is already loose, uncompacted, pre-broken, or can be picked up directly.
- **vs. combination unit:** combination units are strictly wet-work machines; for dry material recovery a dedicated industrial vacuum loader / air mover must be used. An industrial vacuum can become overwhelmed or leak fine mist with massive volumes of liquid-dominant stormwater runoff. For a catch basin packed with compressed, bone-dry sand or massive gravel beds, an industrial vacuum (air mover) or a heavy-duty liquid vacuum truck with a positive-displacement blower is more efficient than a combo.
- **vs. liquid vacuum:** liquid vacuums handle corrosive, acidic, hazardous and 100% liquid materials that would leak out of or ruin the dry filtration baghouses of an industrial air mover.
- **vs. liquid ring:** industrial vacuums use dry filtration baghouses that get blinded and ruined by heavy moisture, and can generate static sparks; liquid rings handle high-moisture, high-vapor streams and volatile gases safely. Choose an industrial vacuum for dry powders or damp debris.
- **with roll-off boxes:** an industrial vacuum truck must leave the site to dump once its tank fills; staging roll-off boxes (including vacuum-rated boxes it can load directly) keeps it working on site.
