# Trailer

## Summary

Trailer is a form factor, not a function. Vac2Go's trailer units are hydro excavation trailers, a jetter trailer and a vacuum excavator trailer (two-box roll-off trailers sit under Roll-Off). Trailers suit tight access (alleys, residential areas, parking garages), small-volume and maintenance jobs, and projects where the unit stays parked on site for weeks. Compared with truck-mounted units they use smaller hoses (2 to 3 inch maximum particle size), carry less water and debris, and recovered material leaves by towing the trailer or by pumping into a staging tank. The customer supplies a suitable tow vehicle. Most rental trailers are not built for hazardous, regulated or flammable material. Move up to a truck when volume, hose distance or continuous production grows.

### Group A: Trailer, high level

**Q: Trailer is a form factor rather than a function. Which trailer types are available: jetter trailer, hydro excavation trailer, vacuum trailer, roll-off trailer, others?**

Vac2Go's trailer units are hydro excavation trailers (Bossvac Hydrovac Trailer, Kaiser Premier TerraVac), a jetter trailer (GapVax Combo G7 Trailer Jetter), and a vacuum excavator trailer (Vermeer LP XDT). Ask about site access and volume first, then point the customer to the right type.

**Q: What kinds of jobs are trailer units used for?**

Residential utility potholing, localized municipal pipe cleanouts, facility indoor maintenance, parking garage catch-basin cleaning, and small-volume fluid transfers.

**Q: Beyond municipal work and oil & gas, which industries most commonly rent trailer units?**

Electrical contracting, residential plumbing, municipal public works, and property management.

**Q: Can trailer units handle both wet and dry material, and does the answer vary by trailer type?**

Hydro excavation and jetter trailers are wet-only. Specialty dry vacuum trailers can handle dry material, but most rental trailers are configured for wet slurry and liquid extraction.

**Q: Trailer and portable vacuums are better for maintenance work, confined spaces, and small volumes. Where is the practical crossover point: material volume, hose distance, job duration, or site access?**

The practical crossover point to upgrade to a truck-mounted unit is when material volume exceeds 500 to 1,000 gallons, when hose distances exceed 100 feet, or when a project requires continuous production over multiple shifts without immediate on-site dumping access.

**Q: What can a trailer unit do that a truck-mounted unit can't: site access, indoor or interior use, staying on site for the duration?**

Trailers offer unmatched tight site access, can navigate low-clearance indoor parking structures, and can be left parked on-site for weeks as a stationary tool without tying up an expensive truck chassis.

**Q: What can a trailer not do that a truck can? In particular, how does recovered material leave the site?**

Trailers have low capacities. Recovered material leaves the site via the trailer itself (requiring towing it to a dump site) or must be pumped out into a larger staging tank on-site.

**Q: What tow vehicle do I need: GVWR, hitch type, brake controller? Is a tow vehicle available as an add-on?**

Typically requires a minimum three-quarter-ton or one-ton truck with a minimum Class IV/V hitch, a 14,000 to 20,000 lb GVWR towing capacity, and an integrated electric brake controller. Vac2Go does not typically supply the tow vehicle; the customer must provide a qualified truck.

**Q: Is a CDL required to tow any of the trailer units?**

If the gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) of the trailer exceeds 10,000 lbs and the combined truck/trailer weight exceeds 26,000 lbs, a CDL is required to legally tow it on public highways.

**Q: What jobs is a trailer unit not the right choice for?**

High-volume main line sewer clearing, large-scale deep foundation daylighting, or long-distance cross-country bulk waste hauling.

**Q: What are the most common reasons customers rent a trailer unit?**

Limited physical space on the job site, and a smaller setup than a full-size truck for small, localized projects.

### Group D: Material handling: Trailer

**Q: For each trailer type, how does the material envelope differ from its truck-mounted equivalent?**

Trailers use smaller 3-inch or 4-inch vacuum lines (compared to the 8-inch truck lines), drastically limiting the size of solids they can safely clear.

**Q: What is the maximum particle size and debris weight for a vacuum or hydro trailer?**

Maximum particle size is restricted to 2 to 3 inches. Heavy rocks will quickly lodge in the smaller hose bends.

**Q: Can trailer units handle abrasive material, and does wear become a bigger concern on a smaller unit?**

They can handle abrasive sand and grit, but because the tank walls and cyclone separators are thinner than those on full-size trucks, internal abrasive wear happens much faster and requires frequent monitoring.

**Q: Can they handle hazardous or regulated material?**

No; most rental trailers lack the stringent DOT code structural engineering required to legally haul hazardous or regulated waste on public roads.

**Q: Can they handle flammable or combustible material?**

Compact trailer units lack complex grounding systems and explosion-proof engines, creating an immediate ignition hazard for flammables.

**Q: Are there materials that are acceptable on a truck-mounted unit but not on the trailer version?**

Large structural cobblestones, thick unpumpable crude sludges, and certified hazardous chemical waste.

**Q: What is the water capacity on a jetter or hydro trailer, and how long can it run before it needs a refill?**

Jetter or hydro trailers typically hold 200 to 400 gallons of water, providing roughly 30 to 45 minutes of continuous digging/jetting run-time before needing a fresh water refill.
Advisor note: Unit literature differs: the GapVax G7 Trailer Jetter brochure shows 500 or 700 gallon tanks. Use the unit's own figures when a specific unit is discussed.

### Group E: Sizing and "is this the right truck": Trailer

**Q: How do I know if I need a trailer unit vs. a truck-mounted unit?**

Choose a trailer if you have severe height/width access limits or low budget allocations for a quick job; choose a truck if daily production and high debris volume are your priorities.

**Q: What drives the sizing decision for trailers: tank capacity, jetter pressure and flow, engine size, or towability?**

Driven primarily by towability (weight limits) and debris tank capacity.

**Q: Can a trailer be left on site for the duration of a project? Is that a common rental pattern, and does it change the rental structure?**

Yes, leaving a trailer stationary on a construction site for the duration of a multi-week project is a very common rental pattern. It does not change the structural rental rate, but it eliminates daily travel wear and tear.
