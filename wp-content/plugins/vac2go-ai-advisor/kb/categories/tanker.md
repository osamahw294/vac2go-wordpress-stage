# Tanker

## Summary

A tanker is a large semi-trailer barrel for high-volume liquid transport over public highways, either transport-only (loaded by a vacuum truck or plant pump) or self-contained with its own pump. Vac2Go's tankers are 130 BBL units. They carry pumpable liquid and light sludge, not heavy solids. Typical uses: large tank evacuations, pond remediation, drilling fluids and long-distance hazardous waste transport. On heavy products, legal road weight usually limits usable capacity before volume does. Tankers can be rented alone or with a tractor; the customer's driver needs the right CDL endorsements and the customer handles placarding and manifests. For on-site extraction of high-solids material a liquid vacuum truck fits better, often with tankers cycling to disposal.

### Group A: Tanker, high level

**Q: What is a tanker: a vacuum tanker with its own pump, a transport-only tanker pulled by a tractor, or both?**

A tanker encompasses both options: transport-only tankers (vacuum-rated but requiring an external tractor/pump to load) and self-contained vacuum tankers equipped with their own onboard auxiliary donkey-engine pumps.

**Q: What kinds of jobs are tankers used for?**

Large-scale chemical plant tank evacuations, massive environmental pond remediation projects, hauling bulk drilling fluids, and long-distance hazardous waste transport.

**Q: Which industries most commonly rent them?**

Oil & gas production, chemical manufacturing, bulk liquid logistics, and emergency environmental response.

**Q: Can a tanker load material under its own power, or does it need another unit to load it?**

Transport-only tankers cannot load independently and require a vacuum truck or plant pump. Specialized vacuum tankers equipped with an onboard engine and pump can load material under their own power.

**Q: How is a tanker different from a liquid vacuum truck?**

A liquid vacuum truck carries a smaller payload (approx. 2,000 to 3,000 gallons) but is highly agile. A tanker holds a massive payload (approx. 5,000 to 6,300 gallons) but requires a large semi-tractor turn radius and flat ground to operate safely.
Advisor note: These are general figures. For a specific unit use its own literature: Vac2Go's 130 BBL tankers are 5,460 gallons, the Huber Dominator is 3,000 to 3,300 gallons, and the Imperial unit is 5,000 gallons.

**Q: Is barrel capacity how tankers are usually specified, and what sizes are available?**

Yes, barrel capacity is how customers ask for these units. Vac2Go's tankers are 130 BBL units.

**Q: Can a tanker carry solids or sludge, or strictly pumpable liquid?**

Tankers can carry light, pumpable sludge, but they cannot carry heavy solids or non-flowable cake sludges because they lack the hydraulic dump bodies or full-opening rear doors found on liquid vacuum trucks to clean out settled material.

**Q: What can a tanker carry or do that other units can't?**

Highest legal liquid volume payload capacity per trip over public highways.

**Q: What jobs is a tanker not the right choice for?**

Tight urban maneuvering, cleaning out heavy, settled sand traps, or precision utility daylighting.

**Q: What are the most common reasons customers rent a tanker?**

Maximizing transport efficiency and minimizing total transport costs on high-volume liquid transfer projects.

**Q: Are tankers rented on their own, or always paired with a tractor?**

They are rented solo or paired.

### Group D: Material handling: Tanker

**Q: Which products are the tankers approved to haul, and how does a customer confirm compatibility before the rental?**

Approval depends on each tank's rating and certification. Confirm compatibility for a specific product with a Vac2Go rep before the rental.

**Q: Can a tanker haul hazardous or regulated material? What placarding, manifesting, and driver qualification is involved, and who carries that responsibility?**

Yes, they are highly rated for hazardous waste haulage. The customer carries all responsibility for proper DOT placarding, manifest logging, and ensuring the driver holds a valid CDL with a Tanker and Hazmat endorsement.

**Q: Can it haul flammable or combustible liquid? How does the DOT 407 vs. DOT 412 distinction play out across the fleet?**

Yes, if it's rated for it.

**Q: Can it haul acidic or corrosive material? Are lined or stainless tanks available?**

Yes, if it's rated for it.

**Q: Can it carry hot liquids? What are the temperature limits?**

Yes, if it's rated for it. Standard limits are capped at 140°F (60°C) to protect internal tank liners and valve gaskets unless a high-temperature structural spec is requested.

**Q: Can it carry sludge and slurry, or only pumpable liquid?**

They can carry light slurry, but if the product settles during a long transit, it will create a massive blockage behind the internal baffles that is incredibly difficult to wash out.

**Q: Does the tank need to be cleaned between products, who handles that, and what is the policy on prior-product residue?**

Tanks must be returned chemically clean. Vac2Go enforces a strict "clean for clean" policy; any prior-product residue or heel left in the tank will result in substantial commercial cleaning surcharges billed back to the customer.

**Q: Is there a potable-rated tanker in the fleet?**

No. The fleet consists of industrial/environmental transport units; there are no certified potable-rated clean drinking water tankers available.

**Q: Are there materials that should never go into a tanker?**

Class 1.1 explosives, radioactive materials, or un-stabilized chemicals that undergo exothermic polymerization.

### Group E: Sizing and "is this the right truck": Tanker

**Q: How do I know if I need a tanker vs. a liquid vacuum truck?**

Choose a liquid vacuum truck if you are actively vacuuming localized pits with high solids; choose a tanker if you are transferring thousands of barrels of consistent, pumpable liquid across highways.

**Q: What drives the sizing decision: barrel capacity, legal payload weight, or road and site access?**

Sizing is driven by legal payload weight limits and barrel capacity.

**Q: How often does legal weight limit usable capacity before volume does? For a heavy product, what is the realistic payload on a 130 BBL unit?**

Legal road weight limits almost always limit usable capacity before volume does when hauling heavy liquids. For a heavy product (like concentrated 12 lb/gal brine or acid), a 130 BBL unit can realistically only be filled to approx. 80 to 90 BBLs before exceeding the legal Federal bridge gross limit of 80,000 lbs.

**Q: When is a vacuum truck supported by tankers recommended rather than a vacuum truck cycling to disposal?**

Recommend staging a vacuum truck at the extraction source to continuously pull heavy lift muck, discharging it directly into a rotation of three to four transport tankers to maintain a continuous loop to an off-site disposal facility.

**Q: For a job of a given liquid volume, what is the rule of thumb for how many tankers are needed?**

For basic planning, allocate one tanker for every 5,000 gallons of liquid volume to be shifted per trip cycle.
